You stepped off the scale at half a pound over. The commission is giving you two hours to make weight. The corner is looking at you. The opponent is somewhere in the back with their team, already hydrated and eating. The camp put you here, but the next 120 minutes are yours to solve, and the tools you have are half of what you had 24 hours ago because you already used them getting to the number you missed.

Every combat athlete needs a written second-attempt weigh-in protocol before they need it, not after. Missing weight is the moment where the worst decisions of a fighter's career get made — the sauna suit gets zipped on for an hour instead of ten minutes, the diuretic gets pulled out of the bag, the fighter dry-heats for 90 straight minutes and steps back on the scale unable to see the LED. This post is the written 2-hour re-cut protocol. It is not a plan for how to get to weigh-in on time — that is what fight camp is for. It is the emergency plan for the moment fight camp already failed.

## The go/no-go call at the scale

Before you touch a sauna, make the go/no-go call. The re-cut is only viable in a narrow window. Outside that window the correct move is to accept the missed weight, take the purse forfeit or the catch-weight negotiation, and rehydrate normally. Grinding a re-cut you cannot make will damage the fighter, cost them the fight, and cost them their next 2 to 4 weeks of training.

**Go criteria — the re-cut is viable if all of these are true:**
- You are 0.5 to 2.0 pounds over the target on the first attempt.
- You still have functional urine output — you produced any meaningful volume in the past 6 hours.
- Skin turgor and mucous membrane read are not catastrophic — you can see clearly, you are not lightheaded standing at the scale, you can hold a normal conversation.
- Rectal or tympanic temperature at the scale is at or below your personal baseline (i.e., you have not already spiked from the earlier cut methods).
- You have at least 90 minutes on the clock.
- Your corner and the on-site medical are aware and cleared.

**No-go criteria — accept the missed weight if any of these are true:**
- You are more than 2.5 pounds over. The physiologic cost of pulling that in 2 hours exceeds what any 24-hour rehydration can undo.
- You have not urinated in the past 6 hours and cannot produce any volume when you try.
- You are already lightheaded, cramping, or nauseated at the scale.
- Your baseline temperature is elevated (you are already in mild heat strain from the initial cut).
- You have less than 60 minutes on the clock.
- Any commission medical says no.

The 0.5-to-2.0-pound window is where the re-cut math is real. Below that, you are already close enough that the moisture on your gi or your bladder is likely the culprit — dry off, empty the bladder, cut hair if needed, and try again in 5 minutes. Above 2.5 pounds, the water compartments the re-cut would pull from are the ones that keep you conscious in round 1. The middle window is where the protocol below applies.

## The 2-hour timeline

Assume you have a 120-minute window. The protocol runs in three phases: pull, hold, rehearse-and-weigh. Adjust proportionally for a shorter window; do not compress phase 3 to expand phase 1.

**Phase 1 — pull (minutes 0 to 60).** Get in a **hot bath or a sauna with intermittent breaks**, not a sauna suit on a treadmill. The bath and the sauna both hit the goal (transdermal water loss via sweat) faster and with less cardiovascular cost than active exercise in a sauna suit at this point in the week. Sauna suit on a treadmill is what you use in the days before weigh-in, not in the 2-hour re-cut where you are already glycogen-depleted, plasma-volume-contracted, and cardiovascular-strained.

- **Hot bath option:** 104 to 108°F (40 to 42°C), full-body immersion to neck, out every 10 minutes for a 3 to 5 minute cool-down and towel-dry weigh. Three to four total immersion cycles across 45 to 50 minutes.
- **Sauna option:** dry sauna at 170 to 190°F (77 to 88°C), or steam room at 110 to 115°F if that is what is available. In for 10 to 12 minutes, out for 5 to 7 minutes to cool down, dry off, and weigh. Three total sauna cycles across 45 to 50 minutes.

Do not do both. Do not run a sauna suit on top of either. Do not do jump-rope or shadow boxing inside the bath or the sauna. The additive cardiovascular cost is what kills fighters in the emergency re-cut — the news reports of fighters dying in weight cuts are almost always from the compounded methods, not one of them alone.

**Phase 2 — hold (minutes 60 to 100).** Come out, dry off completely, weigh. If you are at target, stop pulling. Sit in a cool room, feet up. Do not drink. Do not eat. Do not take a supplement. Do not chew gum. Do not rinse your mouth with water. Do not shower — a warm shower will make you re-sweat and lose more than the water on your skin, a cold shower will trigger a shiver response that consumes energy you do not have. If you are still not at target, do one more short sauna or bath cycle (10 minutes), come out, dry off, weigh again.

The reason for the hold-still window is that a lot of the total weight loss from the sauna or bath phase shows up in the 20 to 30 minutes after you exit, as your body continues to release stored heat and the vasculature reshuffles. Fighters who step off the sauna, straight onto the scale, and see they need another 0.4 lb often panic into another sauna round when the same 0.4 lb was going to come off during a 15-minute cooldown anyway. Wait, then decide.

**Phase 3 — rehearse and weigh (minutes 100 to 120).** Ten minutes before the re-weigh window closes, do a rehearsal weigh in the room. Dry, naked, no jewelry, empty bladder, empty bowel, empty mouth, hair dry. If you are at or under target, go to the official scale. If you are within 0.1 to 0.3 lb over, cut hair, cut fingernails and toenails, clip anything else on the body that carries mass, dry off again. If you are more than 0.5 lb over at this point, the re-cut did not work — accept the miss and rehydrate.

## What you do not do in the 2-hour window

The re-cut moment is the moment the worst supplement, diuretic, and heat-strain decisions get made. Every one of these has been done by fighters who missed weight, and every one of them made the outcome worse.

**No diuretics.** Not a prescription loop diuretic, not a natural diuretic tea, not dandelion extract, not a pre-workout with a "water loss" claim. Loop diuretics and the natural ones will pull water but they will do it by dumping potassium and sodium as well, which is exactly what you cannot afford entering a fight in 24 hours. Also, most commissions are testing for the pharmacologic ones — a positive is a suspension.

**No sauna suit on top of the sauna or bath.** The additive heat strain drops core temperature regulation past the point where the compensation mechanisms hold. This is the mechanism that has killed fighters in weight cuts. Choose one heat modality per phase, run it in intervals, monitor.

**No exercise in the sauna or bath.** Same reason. You need transdermal water loss, not glycogen depletion — you have none to spare, and the cardiovascular cost of exercise on top of heat is what causes syncope in the sauna.

**No fluid intake.** Not water, not sips, not ice chips, not "rinse and spit." Anything that gets past the lips gets absorbed against the number you are trying to hit. If your mouth is unbearable, spray with a spray bottle, spit fully, do not swallow.

**No food.** Same reason. Even a mint has mass and moisture.

**No hot chair or hot wrap in the last 20 minutes.** These continue to pull after you have made the decision to hold. You will overshoot and rehydrate from a deeper hole than you needed to.

**No panic.** The re-cut window is 120 minutes. Fighters who panic in the first 30 and start compounding methods lose the last 90. Follow the phased plan.

## When you make weight on attempt 2: the rehydration plan is different

Making weight on attempt 2 is a different physiological state than making it on attempt 1. You have run through a second heat exposure on top of an already depleted athlete. Plasma volume is deeper in the hole. Cellular electrolyte gradients are more distorted. Gastric motility is slower. The rehydration plan you rehearsed for a normal weigh-in has to be modified.

**First 30 minutes off the scale:** small volumes only. 4 to 6 ounces of an [electrolyte solution](https://scythene.com/products/electrolytes-berry?utm_source=combatdietitian&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=supplement-links&utm_content=second-attempt-weigh-in-emergency-cut-protocol) with 500 to 700 mg sodium per 8 oz, sip slowly. Do not chug. Do not follow with plain water in the first 30 minutes — the intracellular sodium and osmolality gradients are the fragile part and dumping plain water in on top of a depleted athlete drops serum sodium fast.

**Minutes 30 to 90:** graduated volume, still electrolyte-forward. Continue the electrolyte solution at 8 to 12 oz every 15 minutes as tolerated. Introduce simple carbohydrate (a sports drink, a small amount of fruit, a rice-based carb source) once you have had at least 30 minutes of tolerated electrolyte intake and you can urinate.

**Minutes 90 to 240:** the food comes in. Small, frequent, low-fat, low-fiber. Rice, potato, banana, small amounts of well-cooked lean protein. No large volumes of anything. No high-fat meals — the gastric motility from a second cut is not ready for it and you will regret it in the corner tomorrow. No steak-and-eggs "reward meal."

**Overnight:** continue small carbs and small protein at each check-in with the corner. Weigh at bedtime, weigh at wake-up. The fighter who missed weight and then made it on the re-cut needs to be waking up 1.5 to 2.5 pounds above the target — that is normal, expected, and the sign the rehydration worked. Waking up at or below the target 12 hours before the fight is not a win; it is a fighter who did not rehydrate enough.

Do not try to hit pre-cut hydration levels overnight. That is not the goal. The goal is a fighter who feels stable, urinates a normal color and volume, is not cramping, and can eat a normal pre-fight meal 4 hours before the walk.

## When you do not make weight on attempt 2

If you step on the scale for the second attempt and you are still over, you are done cutting. Do not run a third attempt. Do not go back in the bath. The physiologic damage compounds and the fight tomorrow is what you are supposed to be preparing for, not the scale.

The move at this point is to accept the missed weight and negotiate. The corner and the manager handle the commission conversation. Most jurisdictions require a percentage forfeit of the purse to the opponent (typically 20 to 30 percent for a 0.5 to 2.0-pound miss, more above that). The opponent may accept a catch-weight bout at the fighter's actual weight, may accept with a rehydration cap, or may decline. Whatever the negotiation, the fighter's next move is the same: **rehydrate as if this were a normal successful weigh-in**, on the plan that was already rehearsed. The extra 2 hours of heat exposure means the rehydration needs to be slightly more electrolyte-forward and slightly more cautious with volume for the first 60 minutes, but the goal state (stable, fed, hydrated, at fighting weight) does not change.

The single worst decision after missing on attempt 2 is to also miss the fight tomorrow because the fighter tried to keep cutting or did not rehydrate seriously. Two losses in 24 hours (missed weight plus lost fight from a shelled body) is what happens when the re-cut becomes a third-attempt cut. Do not let that happen.

## The camp lesson that has to happen the next week

The re-cut is a bandage. The camp that put a fighter into a re-cut has a math problem — the pre-camp weight was too high, the cut plan was too aggressive for the fighter's body, or the fight-week hydration and glycogen timing was off. Every re-cut becomes a case file for the next camp. Weigh in the same location, at the same time of day, in the same clothes as the fight-week rehearsals. Track hourly weight the day before weigh-in. If the trend line for weigh-in day is more than 0.5 pounds off the prior day's projection, adjust — a fighter who is going to miss should be identified 24 hours before the scale, not at it.

The re-cut is the tool for the day the trend line was wrong. It is not a training tool, it is not part of the plan, and running it should be the loudest signal in the camp that the plan for the next fight has to change. If the weight is not sustainable at that division without a re-cut, the fighter is in the wrong division — and that conversation between the fighter, the coach, and the sports dietitian is the one that keeps the fighter's career going past the fights where the scale nearly ended it.
